Intensity Balance

Training Intensity Blindness

Most of the interval sessions we think we're doing at low/moderate intensity are actually done at high intensity. Science calls it intensity blindness — and it's the most common mistake in training.

December 20, 2023 6 min read By Mick Di Betta

If you ask any coach or athlete to name the most common training mistake, they'll probably say "paddling too hard on easier intervals" — not getting through the full session at consistent intensities. Science backs this up through countless research studies. In short, most of the interval sessions we think we're doing at low or moderate intensity are in fact done at high intensity. This is what I call intensity blindness.

Spending nearly half of total training time at high intensity wouldn't be a problem if it were an effective way to train — but it isn't. Research has shown that elite endurance athletes in every discipline, from running to rowing, do 80% of their training at low/moderate intensity and the remaining 20% at high intensities. This universal pattern is the result of generations of trial and error at the highest levels of endurance sport. Virtually every approach to balancing training intensities has been tried — only the 80/20 balance has survived, because it works best.

Does the 80/20 Rule Apply to Non-Elite Athletes?

Studies have also demonstrated that athletes of all experience and ability levels improve most when they follow the 80/20 rule of intensity balance. Breaking out of a 50/50 split and shifting toward 80/20 can take time — and it requires lowering your training ego.

Step 1: Find Your Intensity Zones

To spend 80% of training time at low/moderate intensity and 20% at high intensity, you need to know what those intensities actually are for you. Using a five-zone system: Zones 1 and 2 are low intensity, Zone 3 is moderate, and Zones 4 and 5 are high intensity.

Step 2: Plan 80/20 Training Weeks

This is simple maths. If you train 5 hours per week (300 minutes), 80% of that is 240 minutes — 4 hours at low/moderate intensity, leaving 1 hour at high intensity.

Step 3: Monitor and Control Intensity

It's one thing to plan the perfect 80/20 week; another to actually execute it. Many athletes intend to do most training at prescribed intensities but when they get on the water, they do something else — without even realising it. Fixing this requires actively monitoring intensity throughout every interval and staying in the targeted zone.

The RPE advantage: Many athletes get caught up with heart rate monitors and find their training intensities become erratic and time-consuming. A preferred method is the RPE scale (Rate of Perceived Exertion) — it's immediate, free, and always accurate.

Being disciplined about intensity can be surprisingly difficult at first. But if you take the leap of faith and follow through on slowing your easy sessions down, you'll be well rewarded. The first thing you'll notice is less fatigue from day to day. Then you'll find you can train faster and more comfortably in higher-intensity workouts.
