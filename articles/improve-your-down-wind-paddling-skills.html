Improve Your Down Wind Paddling Skills

You don't need Molokai Channel conditions to develop your down wind skills. A simple into-wind and back approach in any onshore conditions will build the stroke timing and board control you need.

December 20, 2023 4 min read By Mick Di Betta

Training for any paddling event requires specific training intensities and the ability to cover volumes approaching the race distance. The first step is designing a Training Plan: find a start date, know your race date, and work out what happens in between.

For an event like Molokai to Oahu, I'd typically start around 1 March and race on 30 July — then build specific training phases within that window to develop the strength and endurance to paddle 32 miles. During those phases, the goal is to progressively add intensity and volume until you have the strength to hold your best pace over the full distance. That's endurance.

The Most Common Down Wind Training Excuse

Every year, the same question comes up: "But I don't get the same conditions as the Molokai Channel, so I can't practice my down wind skills."

Here's the good news: you don't need them.

The Tip That Works

Tip #1 — Into-wind and back: The best way to develop stroke timing and board control for down wind and rough water paddling is to do half your paddling sessions into the wind, then paddle back down wind. The wind conditions don't need to be strong to get the benefits, and the distances don't need to be long.

By repeating intervals into the wind and then coming back, you develop two things simultaneously:

  • Into-wind: strength, stroke efficiency, and the discipline to hold pace against resistance
  • Down wind: stroke timing, board reading, and the ability to accelerate onto and hold runs

This approach works perfectly for paddlers who get onshore wind days during their preparation period and don't need transport up or down the coastline. Even light onshore conditions are sufficient. Get out there when the wind is on — and hone the skills you'll need in rough water racing.

The channel won't practise for you. The preparation period is your window to build the timing, board feel, and confidence that makes the difference when the conditions are genuinely challenging.
