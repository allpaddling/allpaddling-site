Training Metrics

It's You Versus You

Before you can work on paddling fast, you have to find what speed you're capable of paddling at consistently. Here's how to conduct your own FTP field test and use it to set precise training zones.

December 20, 2023 6 min read By Mick Di Betta

Before we could run, we had to learn to walk — and nobody told us exactly how to do that. We worked it out ourselves. Training programs work the same way. They're not telling you to paddle at this speed or that speed; they're giving you the framework to find out what your speed actually is and help you improve it.

So before you can work on paddling faster, you have to find what speed you're capable of paddling at consistently without total exhaustion. That's your starting point. Everything else builds from it.

Why RPE and FTP?

At the top of every training program is a Training Intensity Scale. Whether you use one or more of the available modes is a personal choice — but the best results come from combining RPE (Rate of Perceived Exertion) with FTP (Functional Threshold Pace).

Stroke rate and heart rate are also valid tools, but both have too many variables for precise zone management. RPE and FTP together give higher accuracy in most training conditions.

The Five Training Zones and Why They All Matter

The five training zones aren't just about variety — each one targets a different physiological system. Training at intensities above and below your FTP stresses the central nervous system's tolerance, creates metabolic fatigue, and when done consistently, drives muscular fatigue and energy fatigue. All of these are indicators of training adaptation.

The formula: Stress + Rest = Progress. Emotional fatigue, central nervous tolerance, metabolic fatigue, muscular fatigue, and energy fatigue all need monitoring. When one system isn't recovering, pay more attention to rest.

The FTP Field Test

Find the most sheltered water available — minimal wind and current. Do half your test in each direction to average out any resistance differences.

Warm up 10 minutes, then paddle your 1000m test and record the time. Train back easy to your start. Warm down 5–10 minutes, then paddle the 500m test back over the same water and record the time.

Calculate your FTP (Critical Speed) using this formula:

Critical Speed = (Distance of longer test − Distance of shorter test) ÷ (Time of longer test − Time of shorter test)

Example: 500m test in 3:16 (3.26 min), 1000m test in 6:25 (6.41 min)
Critical Speed = (1000 − 500) ÷ (6.41 − 3.26) = 500 ÷ 3.15 = 158.7 m/min ≈ 9.5 km/h

This result should closely match a 60-minute maximal effort or lab-determined lactate threshold pace. Use it as your TZ3 FTP for training. Retest every 4–6 weeks to track progression and update your zone targets.

From your FTP, your five training zone paces are calculated as percentages — giving you precise individual targets for every interval session. You're not chasing someone else's numbers. You're measuring yourself against yourself. That's where real progression lives.
