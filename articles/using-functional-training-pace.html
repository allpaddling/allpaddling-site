Training Metrics

Using Functional Training Pace

Functional Threshold Pace is the maximum effort you can maintain while your body can still clear the lactate your muscles produce. Here's why it's the cornerstone of endurance training — and how to find yours.

December 20, 2023 7 min read By Mick Di Betta

Lactate Threshold Pace, Heart Rate, or Power — all refer to the same thing: the maximum effort you can maintain while your body can still remove the lactate acid being used by your muscles. For most trained athletes, this is similar to the maximum effort they can maintain for an hour. Going above this threshold will result in a "burn" in the muscles, and after a few minutes, force you to slow or stop until the lactate clears. This is also called Functional Threshold Pace, or FTP.

Why FTP Matters More Than VO₂max

Unlike VO₂max, which relies largely on genetically predisposed physiological characteristics (lung capacity, body mass), lactate threshold can be consistently improved through training. Training both below and above threshold improves the body's ability to clear lactate and to tolerate higher lactate concentrations. As you gain fitness, your threshold rises — and with it, the pace you can sustain for long distances.

Your training zones are all determined as a percentage of your FTP threshold. This makes finding it essential.

How to Determine Your FTP

Lab testing

In a lab, a graded test with blood or oxygen exchange samples identifies the exact effort at which you can no longer clear acid as fast as you're producing it. This is the most accurate method.

Field testing (without a lab)

The next most accurate approach: a maximal 1-hour solo effort. If a full 60-minute test isn't feasible, Joe Friel and TrainingPeaks recommend a 30-minute maximal effort and using the average pace for the last 20 minutes as your lactate threshold estimate.

Important: Field tests should be done in sheltered, consistent conditions — same location, same time, same warm-up protocol each time. Test alone, not with a group that may push you above or below your actual threshold.

Building Stamina Through FTP Training

Stamina in endurance sports is the ability — physically and mentally — to keep performing for a long period. It's measured as a percentage of maximum capacity, with most individuals falling in the 75–85% range.

To build stamina, include 3–5 workouts per training cycle (3–4 weeks) focused specifically on raising threshold performance. These sessions should target RPE 5–6 and Zone 3–4 (just below FTP pace). This approach pushes threshold upward from below — increasing the ceiling while maintaining an aerobic focus.

Monitoring Progression

By combining FTP-based pace targets with stroke rate (as a percentage of your maximum strokes per minute) and RPE, you can monitor training intensity with confidence in almost any conditions. Retest every 4–6 weeks to track increases in pace and adjust your zone targets as your threshold rises.

The goal is simple: over time, the pace that feels like TZ3 today becomes a pace you can hold for longer, more comfortably. That's stamina — and that's what gets you across a 32-mile channel feeling capable rather than depleted.
