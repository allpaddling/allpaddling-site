Training Philosophy

Periodization is Key to Aerobic Power

So much changes in a 32-mile open ocean race — currents, swell, wind, tides. But it's still 32 miles. Plan for the worst, train with a periodised plan, and build the aerobic power to handle all of it.

December 20, 2023 7 min read By Mick Di Betta

So much can change when you set out to paddle 32 miles across an open ocean channel — currents, swell, wind, heat, tides. But it's still 32 miles. Plan for the worst, and hope for the best. Planning for the worst sounds grim — but it gives your periodised training plan a lot more variety, which helps with motivation during those months of preparation leading into your goal race.

What You Can and Can't Control

When paddling an open ocean endurance race, what you can't control: currents, tides, wind, and swell. What you can control: executing a correct periodised training plan to build aerobic power and the mental stamina to not completely fatigue against those elements.

Anyone who has trained long distances will have felt the heaviness in their arms, shoulders, and entire body at some point — whether it's 1 hour in or 4 hours in. Fatigue after 1 hour indicates very poor preparation. Fatigue after 4 hours indicates poor periodisation.

What Is Periodisation?

Simply put: you build a base, then build on that base. The bigger the base, the more you can build. Without an adequate base, you won't develop enough fatigue-resistant muscular endurance to sustain 2, 3, or 7-hour distances.

The Base Period trap: In the base phase, you are preparing to train — not training to race. Many athletes get 4 weeks of training down and then enter every available race, cutting short the base they need for the full season. Or, with no races on the calendar, they don't build a base at all until the calendar changes — leaving themselves underprepared and fatigued.

A Periodised Plan for Endurance Paddling

PhaseDurationFocus
Base Phase6–8 weeksGeneral Preparation (Aerobic Threshold)
Base PhasecontinuedSpecific Preparation (Aerobic Power)
Build Phase12 weeksPre-Competition (Lactate Threshold)
Build PhasecontinuedCompetition (Aerobic Power / Lactate Threshold)
Peak/Taper Phase2 weeksRace Preparation (Lactate Threshold / Aerobic Threshold)

The Build Phase starts 12 weeks before your goal race. Training becomes more specific to the race — intervals that mimic the stresses of the event, building distance that matches it.

The Physiology Behind It

A needs analysis for 2–8 hour open ocean endurance paddling identifies three physiological ways to improve fitness: Aerobic Capacity, Aerobic Power, and Lactate Threshold. The faster you can paddle for the longest time, the better your result will be — and that requires the highest Lactate Threshold (Functional Threshold Pace) you can achieve.

Scientific testing has shown that the average athlete has a Lactate Threshold of 70% of their Aerobic Power/VO₂max — but training can raise this to 94% of VO₂max. Improving both Aerobic Power and Lactate Threshold together delivers the biggest gains in threshold speed.

Do a Paddle Needs analysis of your experience — strengths, weaknesses, paddling history, whether you start fast and fade or start conservative and finish strong — and build your periodised plan around what you actually need to develop.
