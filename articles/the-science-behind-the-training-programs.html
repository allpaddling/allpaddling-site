Interval Training

The Science Behind the Training Programs

Why muscle fibre types matter, what the 2×2 Progression method achieves, and how FTP-based pacing gives you precise, individual training targets every session.

December 20, 2023 8 min read By Mick Di Betta

Skeletal muscles are composed of a mix of fibre types: Type I (slow-twitch) fibres are smaller, more efficient, and fatigue-resistant — better suited to aerobic pathways but producing less power and speed. Type IIa and IIx (fast-twitch) fibres are larger, produce more power and speed, but fatigue more easily.

Muscle fibres are recruited in order of intensity: Type I first, then IIa, then IIx. This means that to recruit and train fast-twitch fibres, you must train at higher intensities — otherwise slow-twitch fibres handle all the work and fast-twitch fibres remain undertrained.

Key principle: "A trained muscle fibre is a more aerobic muscle fibre." When trained, Type IIa fast-twitch fibres become more fatigue-resistant. Because they produce greater contractile force than Type I fibres, trained IIa fibres are what drive top-end endurance performance across all paddling distances.

If you're always paddling at moderate pace in TZ3, you're missing out on training fast-twitch fibres and will race below your performance potential.

2×2 Progression: Sprint Endurance and Endurance

All training needs to be progressive — from every single interval to training weeks, months, and years. The 2×2 Progression (2x2P) uses two distinctly different progressive interval sessions to increase Functional Threshold Pace (FTP). By targeting all key energy systems — Alactic Power (5–20 sec), Anaerobic Capacity (30–90 sec), Maximal Aerobic Power (3–7 min), and FTP (20 min+) — and varying repetitions, duration, and rest intervals across a four-week cycle, adaptation is continuous and boredom is eliminated.

FTP-Based Pacing

Once you know your FTP (your threshold speed), calculating training zone targets is straightforward:

ZoneRPE% of FTP
TZ12–3<75%
TZ24–585–90%
TZ35–695–100%
TZ47–8105–110%
TZ59–10>115%

After just a few weeks of interval training using a GPS device and the RPE 1–10 scale, you'll develop a strong feel for pace in each training zone and gain the confidence to push yourself in zones above your current FTP. Retest every 4–6 weeks, track increases in speed, and adjust zone targets accordingly.

Maximal Aerobic Capacity: the "Endurance" Session

VO₂ intervals — very hard efforts for 3–7 minutes — increase aerobic capacity and tolerance to acidosis. One session per week, supplemented with longer sub-FTP mileage, builds the maximal aerobic capacity foundation.

Week 1: TZ1 2min / TZ2 4min / TZ3 1min / TZ4 2min — Repeat ×6
Week 2: TZ1 2min / TZ2 3min / TZ3 1min / TZ4 2min / TZ5 0.5min — Repeat ×7
Week 3: TZ1 2min / TZ2 3min / TZ4 3min / TZ5 1min — Repeat ×8
Week 4: TZ1 3min / TZ2 4min / TZ3 1min / TZ4 2min — Repeat ×5

Anaerobic Endurance: the "Sprint Endurance" Session

Maximal efforts for 20–90 seconds — important for race starts, finishes, and building tolerance to surging and chasing swells in distance racing. When combined with VO₂max intervals, the combination further increases anaerobic capacity.

Week 1: TZ5 0.5min / TZ4 2min / TZ3 1min / TZ2 4min / TZ1 2min — Repeat ×6
Week 2: TZ5 1min / TZ4 1.5min / TZ3 2min / TZ1 2.5min — Repeat ×7
Week 3: TZ5 1min / TZ4 3min / TZ2 3min / TZ1 3min — Repeat ×6
Week 4: TZ5 45sec / TZ4 1min / TZ3 2min / TZ1 2min — Repeat ×8

Most paddling races are endurance events, so endurance training is the primary focus. But to maximise performance potential, intervals are essential. Use the 2×2 Progression over a four-week cycle — and be faster in your next race.
