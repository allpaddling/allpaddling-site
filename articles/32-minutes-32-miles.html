Interval Training

32 Minutes: 32 Miles

To paddle 32 miles at threshold pace requires a specific kind of stamina — built through high-intensity intervals, not just long slow distance. Here's the HIIT framework that builds it.

December 20, 2023 6 min read By Mick Di Betta

Physiologically, holding threshold pace for 32 miles requires a high level of stamina. Sports science experts have varying opinions on how best to achieve the highest level of endurance for 3–8 hour events — but they all agree on this: it must be built through shorter intervals at intensities both above and below the effort you'll sustain in the race.

Breaking Down 32 Miles

If you can hold 10-minute miles in training for one mile, how do you build the stamina to hold 10-minute miles for 32 miles? The answer is an interval training plan that progressively increases total work time per session above your one-mile pace, while adding training volume in the aerobic intensity zones at approximately 70–80%.

Most athletes put all their emphasis into paddling distance — and are reduced to a slow slog after just a few miles. Distance without intensity doesn't build the engine; it just trains you to paddle slowly for longer.

HIIT for Endurance Paddling

High Intensity Interval Training (HIIT) for endurance: short intervals done at 103–105% above threshold pace (your one-hour pace). HIIT intervals break into two types:

Short HIIT

Used in early preparation. Example: 30 seconds on / 30 seconds easy. Add repetitions to increase volume as conditioning improves.

Long HIIT

Built toward as the season progresses. Example: 2–4 minutes on / 2 minutes easy. These drive the stamina adaptations needed for sustained race-pace effort.

The 32-minute target: As you build total HIIT work time per session toward 32 minutes across your season, you'll have peaked your endurance for the 32-mile distance. In your distance sessions, that stamina will allow you to hold your best mile pace for the full distance.

Volume Split

Remember: it's uncomfortable to hold pace for one hour — so to improve one-hour pace and 32-mile pace, the HIIT sessions must be heavy to severe in intensity to produce maximum adaptation.

The target volume split for seasonal training:

  • 20–30% of weekly volume: HIIT (build up to 10–32 minutes total HIIT work per session)
  • 70–80% of weekly volume: Aerobic threshold intervals

These two types of training work together — HIIT raises the ceiling, aerobic threshold work builds the foundation that supports it. Cut either short, and the other can't reach its potential.
