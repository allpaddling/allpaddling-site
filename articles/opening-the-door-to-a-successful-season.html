Training Philosophy

Opening the Door to a Successful Season

After reviewing the athletes who consistently hit their goals versus those who fall short, the same habits appear every time. Five keys that separate those who arrive ready from those who wish they had.

December 20, 2023 6 min read By Mick Di Betta

The off-season is a perfect time to reflect on what went well during the season and what could be improved — without the pressure of heavy training or upcoming races. After years of reviewing my programs and the athletes who follow them, I've noticed clear similarities between those who consistently achieve their goals and those who just fall short.

Advanced Preparation

The athletes that sit down and review their training plan a few days in advance know exactly what is expected — and when. They don't lose time at the start of each interval figuring out the objective. They arrive knowing their numbers.

Athletes should aim to complete 90% of planned training volume and treat the key interval sessions as non-negotiable. The better prepared you are, the bigger the adaptation you get from each session. Preparation is not just about physical readiness — it's about mental clarity before you even get on the water.

Focusing Time

Once it's training time, it's time to focus on the process of training — nothing else. The athletes who can switch off everyday distractions and zone in on their session numbers are the ones who gain the most consistently and achieve their goals every year.

That focus shouldn't be restricted to training. Being able to prioritise your goals and communicate them to your support crew — family, partner, colleagues — helps everyone feel included in the journey rather than competing with it.

Ego Is Not a Training Zone

The most common failing: training above intensity in the lower training zones, then not having the endurance to maintain intensity in the higher zones. It comes from being distracted by the training of those around you — chasing a faster training partner's wheel rather than holding your own prescribed zones.

The hard truth: Athletes who show self-restraint in training are the ones waiting for their ego-driven training partners at the finish line. Know your numbers. Train your zones. The result takes care of itself.

Good Time Management

There are only 24 hours in a day. The most effective athletes manage training efficiently because they've already done the preparation — they know what the session is, what it requires, and they execute it without wasted time. A well-constructed progressive training plan gives you the structure to fit everything into your day without training becoming a burden on your support crew.

Self-Awareness

Successful athletes listen to their bodies. Fatigue levels, mood, motivation — all are signals. The ability to recognise when to take a rest day, without treating it as failure, is one of the most underrated training skills. Taking a rest day is not a sign of weakness. It's advanced preparation for the session after it.

When planning your season, consider how these five habits — advanced preparation, focus, restraint, time management, and self-awareness — could make your training more effective. The door to a successful season is unlocked from the inside.
