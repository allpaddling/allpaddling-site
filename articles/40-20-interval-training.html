40/20 Interval Training

40 seconds of work, 20 seconds of active recovery. A simple ratio that trains your body to utilise and clear lactate faster — allowing you to hold a higher pace for longer in distance racing.

December 20, 2023 6 min read By Mick Di Betta

The goal of 40/20 training is to continue improving FTP (Functional Threshold Pace) — paddling longer distances at higher speeds. It does this by using combinations of different intensities and interval lengths to stress and rest the body's energy systems, driving adaptation to higher workloads.

Many work-to-rest ratios have been used in high-intensity interval training: 20-on/10-off, 30-on/30-off, 40-on/20-off. All work on similar principles. But the key question — REST or not to REST, and in what proportion — is what separates good trainers from great ones. Many good athletes fail to reach their potential because they get the rest in the wrong proportion to intensity and volume.

Why 40/20 Works

During the intense 40-second work period, you'll be close to or at your Lactate Threshold — the point at which muscles begin producing lactate as a by-product of anaerobic respiration. When lactate accumulates faster than it can be utilised, performance collapses: muscles' ability to contract diminishes significantly, forcing you to slow dramatically or stop.

Therefore, you want to train your body to utilise and clear lactate as quickly as possible. The 20-second active recovery allows your heart rate to drop enough to clear lactate, so you can make another quality effort. Over time, your body becomes efficient at this clearance — and you can hold a faster pace for a longer period of time.

The 40/20 Protocol

Basic session (TZ3/TZ2)

Begin with a 2–3 minute warm-up interval at TZ1 (30% below FTP). Then:

  • Paddle 40 seconds at TZ3 (100% FTP pace)
  • Paddle 20 seconds at TZ2 (20% below FTP)
  • At first attempt, continue the 40/20 for 3 minutes
  • Gradually build up to 3–6 minutes per set over subsequent sessions
  • Follow each set with a 2–3 minute active rest at TZ1
  • Total session duration: 30–60 minutes depending on fitness level and training phase

Advanced combined session (TZ4/TZ2 + TZ3/TZ2)

Once you've mastered the basic session, add a higher-intensity set:

  • Begin with 2–3 minutes at TZ1
  • Paddle 40 seconds at TZ4 (5–10% above FTP pace)
  • Paddle 20 seconds at TZ2 (20% below FTP)
  • These sets are more demanding — when adding them alongside TZ3/TZ2 sets, add only 1–4 minutes per set
Progressive planning: Gradually and progressively build up to combining both sets. By planning your training well in advance, you can create variety in your sessions that builds motivation and targets multiple energy systems across each week.

The 40/20 is not just about working hard — it's about working smart. The active recovery is as important as the effort: it's what makes the next effort possible at quality intensity. Get the rest right, and the adaptation follows.
