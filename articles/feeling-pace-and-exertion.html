Training Metrics

Feeling Pace and Exertion

Intensity, volume, and rest — in the right combinations. The three key contributions to adaptation, and why building your aerobic threshold is the foundation everything else depends on.

December 20, 2023 6 min read By Mick Di Betta

Whatever training intensity measure you use, we're all chasing the same thing: improved speed and economy. The confusion usually lies in combining "how hard to go" with "how long" and "how much rest." Three key contributions to adaptation are Intensity (how hard to go), Volume (how long), and Rest/Active Recovery. Getting these three into the correct combinations — and measuring the correct intensity at which they're completed — drives the best adaptation.

To make it simple: we use numbers 1 to 5 (training zones) for the structure, and RPE numbers 1 to 10 to measure the intensity within each zone. Once intensity and zone are established, all that remains is measuring the volume of each interval and session.

Finding Your Starting Point

For endurance paddling from 3 minutes to 300 minutes or more, you need a starting pace. That starting point is TZ3 — your Functional Threshold Pace (FTP): the pace you can hold for 45–60 minutes. You can find it by simply paddling a 45–60 minute maximal effort, or by using an FTP field test. Once you have it, all five training zone paces follow from it as percentages.

The foundation principle: Anyone can train for 1 hour with a reasonable program — but 50% of those athletes would struggle at 2–3 hours, and only 5% would maintain Anaerobic Threshold Pace past 4 hours. The difference is time spent building Aerobic Threshold conditioning. Spend the time in TZ2 and feel the rewards on race day.

Why Aerobic Threshold Matters Most

Building Aerobic Threshold conditioning is the foundation for developing speed over distance. TZ3, TZ4, and TZ5 work increases FTP and Anaerobic Threshold — but the ceiling of that improvement is set by how well you've built your Aerobic Threshold base. Many athletes don't give themselves enough time in the base phase and cut their Aerobic Threshold training short, effectively putting a cap on the aerobic capacity they can reach on race day.

Getting the correct combination of Aerobic Threshold training to balance higher-intensity interval sessions — and continuing to develop Aerobic Capacity and Anaerobic Conditioning across all five Training Zones — takes time and patience. If you want to hold your best FTP for endurance paddling, plan ahead and don't shortcut the aerobic foundation.
